# The limits of falsifiability: 2026 edition

In high-dimensional living systems, the distinctions that matter often cannot survive a low-dimensional measurement. Binary falsification therefore breaks down as a general criterion for biological knowledge. This edition puts that thesis first and builds the argument from neural populations, protein folding and the relations that sustain living organisation.

The structure is sharper and the mathematics clearer. Information retained is measured against a target hypothesis, with sufficient statistics showing exactly when a reduced description preserves the evidence. A joint-activity example makes the stakes concrete: individual and pairwise observations can erase the entire distinction between competing organisations. Mathematical models, numerical approximations and observational access each have their own role in the argument.

The physical discussion follows the experiment from signal to record to memory reset. Conditions appear beside the results they govern. Collective sensitivity and prediction horizons explain how access changes across scales and over time; the biological sections connect those results to mechanisms, communities and self-maintaining organisation.

The conclusion makes the epistemic claim directly: the reach of a test is not the reach of a living system. An explicit indistinguishability criterion anchors the argument, while evidence across scales, mechanistic constraints and interventions establish a broader basis for biological knowledge.

The published article, *BioSystems* **258**, 105608 (2025), [doi:10.1016/j.biosystems.2025.105608](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biosystems.2025.105608), remains the version of record and the one to cite. This separate edition accompanies it.
